# Convergent Global Futures: Do All Centers of Power Seek a Better Future for the World?

Abstract

Do all major centers of global power genuinely seek a better future for humanity and the world, or does each power center primarily pursue a future aligned with its own strategic interests, security imperatives, and geopolitical objectives?

This question constitutes the first foundational inquiry of Research Cluster I. It examines the potential gap between the declared pursuit of a better global future and the strategic behaviors through which different centers of power attempt to shape that future.

The central premise is that the concept of a “better future” may not possess a universally shared definition. Different power centers may simultaneously seek stability, prosperity, security, technological advancement, and peace while maintaining fundamentally different—and potentially incompatible—conceptions of the global order required to achieve them.

Accordingly, the research asks whether the principal challenge facing the future of humanity is a lack of willingness to pursue a better world, or rather the absence of a shared systemic framework capable of reconciling competing definitions of what constitutes a better future.

This question therefore serves as an entry point for examining the relationship between power, strategic interests, collective action, competing visions of the future, and the possibility of a genuinely shared global trajectory.
